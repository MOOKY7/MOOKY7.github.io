My statistical training is the starting point. This series documents how I translate that training into clear implementations, reproducible experiments, and concise technical explanations.

This entry is a plan for the week beginning October 12, 2026. The tasks below are planned work. Results and code links will be added after the work is complete.

## One main deliverable

Implement a small attention module, check its behavior, and write a short explanation of every tensor shape. Keep the first example small enough to inspect by hand.

## The weekly work

| Day | Focus | Evidence to produce |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Arrays, indexing, and shape reasoning | A notebook of small examples and expected outputs |
| Tuesday | Attention scores and stable softmax | A minimal implementation with shape checks |
| Wednesday | Masking and batched inputs | Tests for visible and masked positions |
| Thursday | Read an architecture paper closely | An explanation of its objective and assumptions |
| Friday | Explain and review | A short technical walkthrough and a list of errors fixed |
| Weekend | Revisit difficult questions | A weekly review with working code links |

## Daily coding practice

Use a small number of problems to practice a full explanation. State the input assumptions. Work through a simple case. Describe time and space cost. Test an edge case before calling the solution complete.

For the first week, the topics are arrays, hash maps, and two pointer methods. The goal is to solve and explain a small set well enough to reproduce the reasoning later.

## Research discussion practice

Prepare a three minute explanation of one current project.

1. What problem does the project address?
2. Why is the obvious baseline insufficient?
3. What changes in the model or evaluation?
4. What evidence would change my conclusion?

The explanation should work for both a statistical audience and a research engineer. The assumptions stay the same. The examples and level of detail can change.

## Review at the end of the week

Record what was completed, link the code, and show one failure that changed the implementation. Move unfinished work to the next plan with a specific reason. A plan is useful only if the review is honest.
